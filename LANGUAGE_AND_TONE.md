# How we write: language and tone

Every page, document, email and screen in this repo follows this guide. It
was set on November 2, 2026, when we rewrote the SNAP E&T journey for the
Vehicle of Service so that a partner, a funder or a participant could follow
it on the first read. For research briefs, `RESEARCH_BRIEF_GUIDE.md` adds the
sourcing and page structure; this guide covers how the words read.

**The short version: plain English, not dumbed down. Say what happens, who
does it, and what the person sees, in words they would use themselves.**

## Plain English, not simple ideas

- **Explain a program before you use its name.** "SNAP Employment and
  Training (E&T) is free job training and job-search help for people on SNAP.
  A nonprofit partner runs it, and the government pays back half the cost."
  After that, "E&T" is fine.
- **Say what a rule means, not just its name.** Not "the time limit applies"
  but "SNAP's work rule applies: 80 hours a month of work or training, or SNAP
  stops after three months." Keep the citation beside it.
- **Use the reader's words, not the agency's.** If a word needs a glossary,
  use a plainer word instead (see the table below).
- **Short sentences, one idea each.** Lead with the point, then the reason.
- **Keep every fact, number, rule and link.** Plainer words never mean fewer
  facts. If the plain version loses accuracy, keep the precise word and
  explain it in the same sentence.

## Describing a journey and each step of a flow chart

- **Name each step by what the person does:** "Find out what may help",
  "Apply for SNAP and ask to join", "Meet and make a plan", "Take part each
  month", "Keep SNAP and move on". Not "Find and screen" or "Assessment".
- **Each flow chart box has three parts:** an action title, then one or two
  short lines saying how and who. "Short SNAP application · name, address,
  signature · starts the clock on day 1."
- **Say who decides, every time a decision happens.** "Only the county can
  sign someone up." Decisions by the county or state sit in their own color.
- **Say when.** "Days 1 to 30", "Within 15 to 30 days", "Every month".
- **A label above a quote says when it appears, in words.** Not "Only when
  the 7-day screen is positive" but "Only if the answers point to emergency
  SNAP", followed by one sentence explaining what that is.
- **Captions say what the colors and boxes mean**, never repeat the chart.

## Words to swap

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| 7-day screen, expedited screen | a check for emergency SNAP (some households can get SNAP within 7 days) |
| minimum application | a short SNAP application (name, address and signature) that starts the clock |
| refer, referral | sign someone up; the sign-up |
| re-screen | check again |
| roster, session roster | attendance, taken once per class |
| case-management contact | a check-in with the case manager |
| employability plan, IEP | a plan for work or training |
| supports, supportive services | help with rides or childcare; help with costs |
| exceptions-only reply | the county replies only about changes |
| provider determination | telling the county a program isn't a good fit for someone |
| good cause | a good reason, such as illness |
| authorized representative | a staff member applying on someone's behalf |
| flow-down terms | data-sharing terms that also cover Refresh |
| consent toggles | permissions chosen one by one, changeable later |
| non-federal money | money that doesn't come from the federal government, such as store profits or foundation grants |
| claims 50% from USDA | asks the federal government (USDA) to pay back half |
| in-kind value | donated help |
| batch, data feed | a file passed between systems each night |
| ABAWD | an adult without children at home, the group SNAP's work rule applies to |

## Screen copy: what the person reads in the app

- **No promises we can't keep.** "Based on your answers, you may be able to
  get SNAP within 7 days. The county will decide." Never a date the county
  hasn't set.
- **Show what has happened, then what comes next.** "We sent your SNAP
  application on [date]. That date counts. Next: your interview with [county]
  Job and Family Services."
- **Never show a number alone.** "This month: [n] hours so far. Thursday's
  class adds 6. Tap to talk with [name]."
- **Information, never pressure.** No "act now", no rewards for enrolling in
  a benefit.
- **Name the person's choices.** "You can change any of these later."

## Tone

- **Abundance and reciprocity**, from Adrian's standing rules: what grows
  when we give, not what is lost if we don't. "Every renewal kept is a
  benefit kept." Close with an invitation, not a warning.
- **We, not they.** "We build that core once." Use "Refresh" when naming the
  product.
- **Confident about what we measured, honest about what we haven't.** State
  measured facts plainly; mark estimates as estimates and say what they rest
  on. Uncertainty goes in "What we will confirm next".
- **People first.** "A returning citizen", "the person", never a label or a
  case number.

## Standing rules that travel with this guide

- **Headings are one plain question**, under ten words, sentence case, with
  no colon. Reference sections are labels ("Sources").
- **Never name a system** a customer's data comes from; name the data and
  who provided it.
- **Never cite or reveal Goodwill Exchange content.** Say "from our
  experience with Goodwill".
- **Names:** the product is Refresh's Vehicle of Service (then "the Vehicle
  of Service"); the division is the Refresh NPO Division; REACH is only the
  nonprofit grant.
- **Brand:** Brand Purple and Melon, lavender surfaces, Outfit headings and
  DM Sans body, sentence case, no em-dashes (`Refresh_Brand_System.md` in
  OneDrive, AI Workspace Products).
- **Team documents** also go out as a Google-Docs-ready Word file, built with
  `Vehicle of Service/Word template - NPO Division/`.

## Read it aloud before it ships

1. Could a partner's front-desk staff member follow every step on the first
   read?
2. Does every flow chart box say what happens, how, and who?
3. Does every decision say who makes it?
4. Is any agency term left unexplained? Check it against the table above.
5. Is any number shown without its source or its next step?
6. Would you be comfortable reading it aloud to the person it describes?
