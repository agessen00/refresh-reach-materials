SNAP E&T with Refresh’s Vehicle of Service

SNAP Employment and Training (E&T) is free job training and job-search help for people on SNAP. A nonprofit partner runs it, and the government pays back half the cost. This plan follows one person through it, and follows the money back to the partner.

The backbone is a loop: the county signs someone up, the partner records their hours, the county confirms they were on SNAP that month, and the partner gets paid. Every screen the person sees helps one of those steps. The county and the state still decide who qualifies, sign people up and pay.

STEP 1 · FIRST CONTACT

Find out what may help

The screen says"Free job training and help finding work. You may also get help with rides and childcare once you have a plan. The county decides who qualifies. We help with every step."
On the first day"Need food today? Here’s what’s open near you. You can apply for SNAP even without a fixed address."
Only if the answers point to emergency SNAPHouseholds with very little money can get SNAP within 7 days. When someone’s answers suggest that, the screen adds: "Based on your answers, you may be able to get SNAP within 7 days. The county will decide."
  • A text message opens the questions on the phone: no app to download, no password to remember, and staff can help if the person loses access
  • Help for today comes from the local 211 or food bank listings, not a list we keep ourselves
  • For someone just released: help getting a state ID, a Social Security card and a birth certificate, and a reminder that Medicaid restarts at release
The app gives information and never pressures anyone to apply; pressure tactics can’t be paid for with SNAP money (7 CFR 277.4(b)(5)). Staff time before someone joins the program is marked as not billable.
STEP 2 · DAYS 1 TO 30

Apply for SNAP and ask to join

The screen says"We sent your SNAP application on [date]. That date counts. Next: your interview with [county] Job and Family Services."
Then: "Your request to join job training was sent on [date]."
  • A short SNAP application with only a name, address and signature is enough to start, and benefits count from the day it is sent. The person sends it on the state’s benefits website, or the partner faxes or emails it and keeps a dated receipt
  • To join job training, the person asks on the same state website, and the partner sends one email to the county. No account on the state website? Staff can help in person, or the person can call 1-844-640-6446
  • The person chooses, one by one, what they agree to: letting the partner check their SNAP, sharing with each named organization, and getting texts. They can change any of these later
Only the county can sign someone up for the program (7 CFR 273.7(c)(2)). The app shows what has already happened, never a date the county hasn’t given.
STEP 3 · WITHIN 15 TO 30 DAYS

Meet and make a plan

The screen says"Your plan with [case manager]: [activity] on [days]. Help with rides or childcare is set in your plan."
  • First a short questionnaire on the state website, then a meeting with a case manager: within 15 days for adults without children at home who aren’t working, and within 30 days for everyone else
  • The case manager writes the plan for work or training in the state’s system
  • The plan’s dates, and parole appointments if the person adds them, show in one calendar so they don’t clash
STEP 4 · EVERY MONTH

Take part each month

The screen says"This month: [n] hours so far. Thursday’s class adds 6. Tap to talk with [name]."
  • The instructor takes attendance once per class, and the person confirms their hours
  • A check-in with the case manager each month, in person, by phone or by email
  • Receipts for rides or childcare go in as a photo; bus passes the partner bought are entered by staff
Adults without children at home need 80 hours a month of work or training to keep SNAP (7 CFR 273.24), and their meetings with the case manager can be no more than half of those hours (OAC 5101:4-9-04). The app never shows the hours on their own; it always shows the next step.
STEP 5 · MONTH 6, MONTH 12, AND AFTER

Keep SNAP and move on

The screen says"Your SNAP renewal is due [date]. Here’s what to gather." For Medicaid: "Watch for your letter. Most Medicaid renewals happen without a form."
  • A short update at month 6 and a full renewal at month 12; a photo of the letter sets the date and the checklist
  • Keeping count of the months that count toward the work rule, finding a job, and a warm goodbye when the person leaves the program
  • From January 2027, a letter listing the person’s hours for Ohio’s Medicaid work rule, ready for the partner to sign. It mostly helps people not on SNAP; for people on SNAP, the state checks this itself

How does the money reach the partner?

The partner pays for the program first, and the government pays back half later. At each step below there is one way money gets lost, and one thing the Vehicle of Service does to stop it.

What can we learn from other states?

Referrals, eligibility checks and hours are moving into state systems; billing mostly is not. In our review of state plans and provider guides, one state lets partners invoice in its shared system. The rest take spreadsheets, templates and secure email.

Beside Ohio, not against it. Ohio is starting design sessions to link a person’s request to join E&T to their open or pending SNAP case, inside the state worker’s system, with an eligibility worker still reviewing each request (FY2026 plan, p. 6). No due date was found. The provider portal is optional and shows assignments and failures (p. 20). So the Vehicle of Service sends people to the state’s request, shows them where it stands, and does the partner’s work the state system leaves out: attendance, the monthly list and the invoice.

Where states have strong systemsFeed them cleanly, with the state’s own fields and statuses. Add what they leave out: the person’s experience and the partner’s billing.
Where states have littleBe the partner’s one place for sign-ups, monthly checks, attendance and billing, sending everything in the state’s exact list and invoice formats.
What we never duplicateDeciding who qualifies, the work-rule status, problem reports where the state website takes them, and the state’s own results figures.

Since October 1, 2025, states report E&T outcomes each year by April 15: jobs and median wages after completion, credentials and skill gains, by participant group (89 FR 90547). FNS collects totals, not case records, so states need clean partner records to build them; our exports can carry the fields the state counts. Nonprofits with a letter from their state SNAP agency could apply for the FNS process and technology improvement grants; the FY2026 round has closed.
