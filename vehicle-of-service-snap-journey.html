SNAP E&T with Refresh’s Vehicle of Service

The money loop is the spine: referral confirmed, participation logged, month confirmed on SNAP, invoice paid. Every screen the person sees feeds one step of that loop. The county and the state still decide, refer and pay.

What does the person see, step by step?

Dana is a returning citizen, an adult without dependents, so the time limit applies. Maria works 25 hours a week and has a 9-year-old, so it does not. Both take the same path; Dana’s hours matter more.

STAGE 1 · FIND AND SCREEN

See what may fit

The screen says"Free job training and help finding work. Help with rides and childcare may be available once your plan is set. The county decides who qualifies; we help with every step."
On day 1"Need food today? Here’s what’s open near you. You can apply without a fixed address."
Only when the 7-day screen is positive: "Based on your answers, the county may approve SNAP within 7 days of your application date."
  • A text link opens the screen on the web: no download, no password, recovery through partner staff
  • Food and day-1 help come from the local 211 or food bank listings, not a list we keep ourselves
  • Screens for likely eligibility, including the 7-day expedited screen
  • For someone just released: state ID, Social Security card and birth certificate steps, and Medicaid that resumes at release
Information, never pressure: persuasive recruitment is not a reimbursable SNAP cost (7 CFR 277.4(b)(5)). Time in stages 1 and 2 is tagged as not E&T.
STAGE 2 · APPLY AND ASK TO JOIN

Fix the date, send the request

The screen says"Your SNAP application date is [date]. Next: your interview with [county] JFS."
Then: "Your E&T request was sent on [date]."
  • A minimum application (name, address, signature) fixes the filing date; the person files it in the state portal, or the partner faxes or emails it with a dated receipt
  • The request to join E&T goes through the state portal, plus one partner email to the county contact; without a portal account, partner or county staff help in person, or the person asks by phone (1-844-640-6446)
  • Consent as separate toggles: release to check eligibility, sharing with each named partner, texts
Only the county refers to E&T (7 CFR 273.7(c)(2)). The app shows what has happened, never a date the county did not set.
STAGE 3 · ASSESSMENT AND PLAN

Meet, then plan

The screen says"Your plan with [case manager]: [activity], [days]. Supports are set in your plan."
  • The state’s pre-assessment questionnaire in its portal; the assessment within 15 days for unemployed adults without dependents, 30 for everyone else
  • The employability plan is written in the state system by the county or the contracted case manager
  • Plan dates, and parole appointments if the person adds them, in one calendar
STAGE 4 · EACH MONTH

Take part

The screen says"This month: [n] hours from your sessions. Thursday class adds 6. Tap to talk with [name]."
  • Hours come from the session roster: the instructor marks one roster per session, and the person confirms
  • Case-management contact in person, by phone or by email, the methods Ohio’s rule lists
  • Receipts by photo; passes bought by the partner are entered by staff
Adults without dependents need 80 hours a month (7 CFR 273.24), and case management stays under half of their assigned E&T hours (OAC 5101:4-9-04). The count never shows alone, always with the next step.
STAGE 5 · KEEP AND MOVE FORWARD

Keep benefits

The screen says"Your SNAP renewal is due [date]. Here’s what to gather." For Medicaid: "Watch for your notice; most renewals happen without a form."
  • Month-6 report and month-12 renewal, with the date read from a photo of the notice
  • Countable months for adults without dependents, a job, and a warm exit
  • An hours letter for Ohio’s Medicaid work rule, ready for the partner to sign. It serves mainly people not on SNAP: the state already excludes people on SNAP who are subject to its work rule, from its own data

How does the money reach the partner?

The partner spends first and is paid back half. Each step of the loop has one way the money slips away, and one thing the Vehicle of Service does about it.

Signed data-sharing terms that flow down to Refresh come first; until then, the county’s reply stays with the partner, outside Refresh.

What can we learn from other states?

Referrals, eligibility checks and hours are moving into state systems; billing mostly is not. In our review of state plans and provider guides, one state lets partners invoice in its shared system. The rest take spreadsheets, templates and secure email.

Beside Ohio, not against it. Ohio is starting design sessions to link a person’s request to join E&T to their open or pending SNAP case, inside the state worker’s system, with an eligibility worker still reviewing each request (FY2026 plan, p. 6). No due date was found. The provider portal is optional and shows assignments and failures (p. 20). So the Vehicle of Service sends people to the state’s request, shows them where it stands, and does the partner’s work the state system leaves out: the roster, the monthly list and the invoice.

Where states have strong systemsFeed them cleanly, with the state’s own fields and statuses. Add what they leave out: the person’s experience and the partner’s billing.
Where states have littleBe the partner’s batched layer for referral, status, participation and billing, exporting in the state’s exact roster and invoice formats.
What we never duplicateThe eligibility decision, the time-limit status, failure entries where the state portal takes them, and the state’s outcome figures.

Since October 1, 2025, states report E&T outcomes each year by April 15: jobs and median wages after completion, credentials and skill gains, by participant group (89 FR 90547). FNS collects totals, not case records, so states need clean partner records to build them; our exports can carry the fields the state counts. Nonprofits with a letter from their state SNAP agency could apply for the FNS process and technology improvement grants; the FY2026 round has closed.

Which questions do the county and state need to answer?

County Job and Family ServicesWill you contract with a new nonprofit partner mid-year, and is it one contract per county? Would you take one status list a month and reply with exceptions only? What invoice format and time records do you need, and how are participant supports paid?
County Job and Family ServicesCan a flat software fee sit in the partner’s E&T budget? What data-sharing terms flow down to a software subcontractor? Is there a cap on households per authorized representative?
Ohio Department of Job and Family ServicesWhen does the automatic link from the portal’s E&T request to the SNAP case go live, and can partners see its status? Does Ohio accept electronic signatures on the minimum application, and does it hold the pre-release filing waiver?
USDA Food and Nutrition ServiceIs there guidance after February 2025 on automation used by third-party assisters? Can household consent release SNAP data beyond 7 CFR 272.1(c)?
Ohio Department of MedicaidWill the SNAP work-rule exclusion apply automatically each month, and will ODM accept organization letters produced from a digital hours log?
